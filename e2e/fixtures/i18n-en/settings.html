▲Higher: more front grip in fast corners, more drag · ▼Lower: less drag, more understeer at speed
▲Higher: a steadier rear in fast corners, lower top speed · ▼Lower: higher top speed, a looser rear in fast corners
▲Higher: fronts lock first: stable braking, more entry understeer · ▼Lower: sharper turn-in on the brakes, rear lock-up risk
▲Higher: better traction out of slow corners, more exit understeer · ▼Lower: freer rotation on throttle, more inside-wheel spin
▲Higher: calmer when lifting and turning in · ▼Lower: more rotation when you lift, less stable entry
▲Higher: sharper response, less grip over bumps and kerbs · ▼Lower: rides kerbs better, slower response
▲Higher: quicker rear response, less traction over bumps · ▼Lower: more rear traction, slower response
▲Higher: less roll, more understeer · ▼Lower: more front grip mid-corner, more roll
▲Higher: less roll, more oversteer · ▼Lower: more rear grip mid-corner and on exit
▲Higher: safer over kerbs, less floor grip · ▼Lower: more floor grip, more bottoming
▲Higher: more rake: keener front, less stability · ▼Lower: more stable, less rotation
▲Higher: quicker steering, less peak grip · ▼Lower: more grip, lazier steering, more heat
▲Higher: livelier rear, less traction · ▼Lower: more traction, more heat build-up
▲Higher: less camber: better braking, less mid-corner grip · ▼Lower: more camber: more mid-corner grip, less braking grip
▲Higher: less camber: better traction, less cornering grip · ▼Lower: more camber: more cornering grip, less traction
▲Higher: toward toe-in: calmer straights, lazier turn-in · ▼Lower: toward toe-out: sharper turn-in, nervous straights
▲Higher: more toe-in: a planted rear, more drag and heat · ▼Lower: less toe-in: freer rotation, less stable
