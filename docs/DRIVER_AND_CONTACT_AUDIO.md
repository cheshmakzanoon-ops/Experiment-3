# Articulated driver and contact-driven audio

## Driver motion

The driver is no longer two rigid sleeves rotating around the steering column. Each shoulder is fixed in chassis-local space, and an analytic two-bone solve places the elbow while the wrist follows the actual steering-wheel transform. Upper and lower lengths are 0.37 and 0.36 metres. An outward/downward pole keeps the elbow in the cockpit. Unreachable targets are exposed in diagnostics rather than hidden by bone stretching.

Original glove geometry separates the palm, grip padding, curled fingers, cuff, seams and thumb. Two independent paddles respond to observed gear increases/decreases. ERS mode changes produce a brief thumb action. The transient controller resets at a replay seek or large time discontinuity rather than fabricating a sequence of shifts. This is a procedural articulated rig, not a motion-captured or commercially authored character.

## Sound consumes actual contact state

The production contact graph separates asphalt, grass, gravel, kerb, tire scrub, water spray, ambient rain, underside contact, collision and gearbox transients. Loaded wheel slip work controls scrub amplitude; lockup and wheelspin change the spectral target. Flat spots and punctures excite a periodic low-frequency waveform at the weighted actual wheel-rotation rate. A stopped wheel has no moving-contact gain. Airborne wheel rotation cannot generate road, tire scrub or spray sound.

Kerb excitation depends on positive changes in normal contact load, followed by an acoustic decay envelope; it is not a timer that plays kerb noise wherever the car happens to be. Underside noise consumes actual skid-contact power. Hybrid whine requires measured motor or regeneration power, not merely a nonzero battery indicator. Three engine RPM bands now have distinct harmonic spectra and torque-dependent filtering. All sounds are generated by this code; no extracted game samples are used.

Audio lifecycle cleanup is idempotent for contact sources and clears old voices, gains and timing before the main audio controller can be reopened. Regular playback still requires the user gesture handled by the application.

## Tests and evidence

Four rig tests cover fixed limb lengths throughout wheel rotation, anchored shoulders, actual shift/ERS transitions, replay discontinuities and degenerate IK geometry. Five audio-state tests cover separate surface routes, airborne guards, slip-based rather than pedal-based scrub, physical flat-spot frequency and actual contact/electrical power.

The exact contact graph also runs in a real Chromium `OfflineAudioContext`, not mocked audio nodes. Four one-second, 48 kHz signal fixtures are generated and measured. Grass and gravel produce distinct high-frequency energy; airborne contact is silent; flat-spot sound has nonzero energy; every sample is finite and the measured peak remains below one. The retained report is `contact-audio-signals.json`. Offline rendering tests signal generation, not speakers, subjective sound quality or audiovisual timing in the full game.

The full-game browser workflow additionally checks both arm chains from a rendered cockpit and retains a cockpit screenshot. This checkpoint does not claim all driver-animation, acoustics, device profiling or whole-project audit requirements are finished.
